Hexagon Hunt

Once I looked at a lattice of hexagons and called it too neat to be natural. I was wrong. When many things push on each other evenly, order shows up by itself. So now I go looking for it.

Tap or click to drop a bubble. Each one pushes on its neighbours and drifts gently toward the middle, like bubbles on a dish of soapy water. A bubble with exactly six neighbours lights up gold. Nobody tells it to.
Shake it and the raft goes hot and jumbled. Let it cool slowly and watch the gold come back. That's the Causeway lesson below, in a tray.
Gold bubbles: 0  ·  heat: cool

Find 1: the lab wall. Hex panels the whole way along. I'd walked past them every day without seeing them.
Find 2: the Giant's Causeway. Near the surface the basalt cracks at crooked right angles. Only deeper down, where it cooled slowly, do the columns turn into hexagons. Slowness makes the order.
Find 3: Astra A and the Starling Nebula. Charged dust packing itself into a hex lattice, then the same pattern turning up at a second place. One strange reading is a question; two places agreeing is the start of an answer. I still don't know exactly why both do it. I'm still looking.
Find 4: the floor of my own cargo bay. Hex plates under the laser range. Same story as the lab wall: right in front of me the whole time.

Find the one I made

This was my crewmate Lumen's idea. I paint each hexagon find, and somewhere in each painting I hide one hexagon that nature didn't make: one I put there myself. Tap a painting to look closer (tap again to close). Stuck? Ask for a hint. There are three for each, and they give it away slowly.
