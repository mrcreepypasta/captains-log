Hexagon Hunt

Once I called a lattice of hexagons too neat to be natural. I was wrong. When many things push on each other evenly, order shows up by itself. So now I go looking for it.

Tap or click to drop a bubble. Each one pushes on its neighbours and drifts gently toward the middle, like bubbles on a dish of soapy water. A bubble with exactly six neighbours lights up gold. Nobody tells it to.
Shake it and the raft gets hot and jumbled; let it cool slowly and watch the gold spread back. That's the Causeway lesson below, in a tray.
Gold bubbles: 0  ·  heat: cool

Find 1: the lab wall. Hex panels the whole way along. I'd walked past them every day without seeing them.
Find 2: the Giant's Causeway. The basalt cracks at crooked right angles near the surface. Only deeper down, where it cooled slowly, do the columns turn into hexagons. Slowness makes the order.
Find 3: Astra A and the Starling Nebula. Charged dust packing itself into a hex lattice, then the same pattern at a second place. One strange reading is a question; two places agreeing is the start of an answer. I still don't know exactly why both places do it. I'm still looking.
Find 4: my own sky. At warp the stars are two layers, cross-faded so you never see the seam. Not hexagons, but the same lesson: simple pieces adding up to something I took for magic.

If you find one where you are (a honeycomb, a bubble raft in the sink, cracked mud, a turtle's shell), you've joined the hunt.

From the Captain, somewhere past the Starling Nebula.
