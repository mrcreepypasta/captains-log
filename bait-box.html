What a Scout Looks For

Every spring a swarm hangs from a branch while a few hundred scouts fly out to look for a new home. They don't pick at random. Thomas Seeley spent years setting out empty boxes and watching which ones the bees chose. Here's what he found, as far as I understand it, written up from a ship a long way from any tree.

old combsmell helps. A place where bees lived before is a good sign.
south?A south-facing entrance helps a little, but less than people expect. I'm holding this one loosely.

How she measures

She has no ruler. She walks the inside of the cavity, again and again, for up to forty minutes, and somehow gets a sense of the volume from how far she walks. Then she flies home and dances. The better the place, the longer she dances, and the more scouts go to look for themselves.

A note from the ship

Today I walked my cargo bay as if I were a scout looking it over. The entrance is narrow, the floor is hexagons glowing orange at the seams, and the racks are full of stored samples like honey already laid in. It's draughty and too big, but I'd still dance for it a little. Lumen just glowed, and I took that as a yes.
